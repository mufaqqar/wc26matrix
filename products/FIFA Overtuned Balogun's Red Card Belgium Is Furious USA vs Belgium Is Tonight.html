Scene 3 — The Appeals Process
Scene 03 How does a red card appeal actually work? And why is it so rarely successful?
Voiceover

The red card appeals process in FIFA tournaments is designed to be difficult. A team has 24 hours to submit a formal appeal after a match. The appeal must include video evidence, written arguments, and sometimes witness statements. A disciplinary committee reviews the case. The standard for overturning a red card is high: the committee must find that the referee made a "clear and obvious error." In 64 years of World Cup football, only one appeal had succeeded before today. Most are rejected within hours. Balogun's appeal was the exception.

Scene 4 — Why FIFA Said Yes
Scene 04 The key evidence that convinced FIFA to overturn the red card
Voiceover

According to sources close to the appeal, three key pieces of evidence convinced FIFA's disciplinary committee. First: the ball was already past the Bosnian defender when contact was made — Balogun was not late, the defender was. Second: Balogun's studs were not showing — his foot was angled downward, not in a dangerous position. Third: the referee's view was partially blocked by another player. FIFA determined that the referee did not have a clear enough view to justify a straight red. The decision was unanimous. Balogun would play against Belgium.
